■ Welcome to Goutokuji (General Temple Guide) - English

Welcome to Goutokuji. Before we begin our journey, let us understand the full picture of this beautiful ancient temple.

Goutokuji is a sprawling sacred sanctuary enveloped in lush, verdant greenery.

The temple's official name is "Daikei-zan Goutokuji," and it was founded in 1480 during the Muromachi period. In 1633, when the Setagaya area came under the domain of the Hikone feudal clan, Lord Ii Naotaka developed deep devotion to the temple and established it as the Ii family's principal place of worship in Edo. The temple's name derives from Naotaka's posthumous Buddhist name, "Kusho-in-dono Gotoku Ten'ei Daiko-ji."

Goutokuji is also widely known as the birthplace of the beckoning cat. According to legend, Lord Naotaka was returning from a falconry excursion when he was beckoned by a white cat at the temple gate. He stopped to visit, and moments later, a fierce thunderstorm broke out. Thanks to the cat's invitation, he was spared from the storm and enjoyed a memorable conversation with the temple's abbot. Deeply moved by this fortunate encounter, the cat was honored as a bringer of good fortune, named "Maneki-neko," and enshrined in the Shofuku-den hall.

Here is your recommended route. Walk straight along the pine-lined path and pass through the historic Sanmon Gate. Once inside the grounds, you will see the Three-Story Pagoda on your left — somewhere within it, three hidden cats are quietly waiting to be found. Ahead of you stands the Buddha Hall, and beyond it to the left, the Shofuku-den, home to thousands of beckoning cats. After paying your respects at the Ii Family Cemetery at the far end, continue clockwise through the Main Hall, the Patriarchs' Hall, the vermilion Red Gate, the historic Bell Tower, and finally the Jizo Hall.

At each spot, you will find real photographs taken on location, along with exclusive historical quiz questions found nowhere else. Answer all quizzes correctly and a special fortune slip awaits you.

Now, smartphone in hand, take a deep breath and begin your pilgrimage.
