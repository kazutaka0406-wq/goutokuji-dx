■ 01_Sanmon Gate - English

The first to greet you is the sturdy wooden Sanmon Gate, erected in 1884 during the Meiji era.
Rebuilt after the Great Kanto Earthquake, it continues to stand with quiet dignity to this day.
The plaque above reads "Hekiunkan" — meaning "the gateway to enlightenment beyond the azure clouds."

In Zen Buddhism, passing through this gate is far more than a simple act of entry.
It is a ritual of purification — a moment to cast off the noise, worries, and illusions of the secular world,
and enter the sacred realm of the Buddha with a cleansed heart and spirit.

One important etiquette tip: step over — never step on — the threshold at the base of the gate.
It is believed that the spirits of the gods and Buddha reside within the threshold,
and treading upon it is considered deeply disrespectful.
Please be mindful as you enter.

Take one deep breath as you pass through.
Then walk slowly down the lush, pine-lined path that stretches before you.

■ 01_Sanmon Gate - Incense Experience - English

In front of the Buddha Hall, you will find a large incense burner.
We encourage you to try the incense experience.

The rising smoke is believed to carry healing power — cleansing physical ailments
and purifying the spirit before standing before the sacred Buddha.
Draw the smoke toward you with your hands and bathe your head and body in it
to arrive before the deity with a pure and open heart.

Incense sticks and a lighter are placed on the left and right sides in front of the burner.

💡 Local etiquette:
Place your incense donation in the offertory box at the front of the Buddha Hall —
not in front of the incense burner itself.
This is the refined, traditional etiquette at Goutokuji.

■ 02_Three-Story Pagoda - English

Completed in 2006, the wooden Three-Story Pagoda rises 22.5 meters into the sky —
a breathtaking presence that commands attention from anywhere in the grounds.

Hidden within this pagoda is the stage for a very special mission:
finding 3 secret beckoning cats concealed somewhere within the structure.

The craftsmen who built this tower playfully carved three tiny cats
into the architecture — waiting quietly to be discovered.
Can you find all three with your own eyes?

This app includes a special feature: photograph the cat you find and check your answer.
If you are having trouble, hints are available to guide you.

■ 03_Buddha Hall - English

Built in 1677, the Butsuden — Buddha Hall — is a designated cultural asset of Setagaya Ward.
Its solemn presence carries the weight of nearly 350 years of history.

Enshrined within are three sacred statues: Amida Nyorai, Shakyamuni, and Miroku Bosatsu —
the Buddhas of past, present, and future — watching over all who visit in quiet serenity.

Standing before these ancient figures, enveloped by the stillness of the grounds,
you may find yourself experiencing a sense of time unlike anything in your daily life.
Please take a moment to press your palms together and offer a quiet prayer.

■ 04_Shofukuden - English

At last — the Shofuku-den, a sight that leaves visitors from around the world utterly breathless.

Thousands upon thousands of pure white beckoning cats line every shelf,
their silent gaze fixed upon all who enter.
What surprises most international visitors is the discovery that not a single cat holds a gold coin.

The Zen teaching of Goutokuji explains it this way:
"The cat brings you the opportunity — the encounter, the chance, the blessing.
But whether that opportunity blossoms into true fortune
depends entirely on your own effort and resolve."

Feel the weight of those thousands of silent gazes upon you.
And quietly, within your heart, make your own vow for the journey ahead.

Beckoning cats (Maneki-neko) are available for purchase at the temple gift shop.

Take your time, and choose with care —

■ 05_Ii Family Cemetery - English

The Ii Family Cemetery is a nationally designated historic site —
a special sanctuary where the air itself seems to change the moment you step inside.

At the far end stands the great tombstone of Ii Naosuke,
the Grand Councilor of the late Edo period.

Facing the imminent threat of Western colonization,
Naosuke courageously concluded the Treaty of Amity and Commerce with the United States in 1858,
bringing an end to more than 250 years of national isolation.
His decision drew fierce opposition, and he ultimately lost his life in the Sakuradamon Incident —
yet his historic courage in opening Japan's gates to the modern world
continues to breathe quietly in this very ground.

Please take a moment to reflect on the weight of this history
and offer a quiet prayer at this sacred place.

■ 06_Main Hall (Hondo) - English

Completed in 1967, the Hondo — Main Hall — is the most sacred structure at Goutokuji,
serving as the very heart of its faith.

Enshrined within is the statue of Sho-Kannon Bodhisattva,
revered for compassionately hearing the suffering and wishes of all people.

Even today, monks continue the tradition of offering daily prayers here,
carrying forward the flame of this temple's teachings.

Grand and resplendent, yet somehow serene and calming —
please take your time to fully absorb the exquisite harmony unique to a Zen temple.

---
■ 07_Patriarchs' Hall (Kaisodo) - English

Standing quietly beside the Main Hall is the Kaiso-do, the Patriarchs' Hall.

Enshrined here are the spirits and ancestral tablets
of the successive high priests who kept the flame of Goutokuji's teachings
burning without interruption across the centuries.

Gazing upon this hall, you come to feel deeply that Goutokuji is no mere tourist destination —
it is a living place of prayer where generations have passed the baton of faith.

Allow yourself a quiet moment to draw close in heart
to the silent, timeless bonds woven across the ages.

---
■ 08_Red Gate (Akamon) - English

The Akamon — Red Gate — paints a vivid vermilion contrast
against the lush greenery of the temple grounds.

Donated directly by the Ii family, the loyal retainer clan of the Tokugawa shogunate,
in 1885, it was originally located at the east gate before being relocated to its current position.

The striking contrast between the surrounding deep green foliage
and the traditional Japanese red lacquer is breathtaking —
making this the most recommended photography spot in the entire grounds.

Please capture a commemorative photograph before this gate of noble, dignified beauty.

---
■ 09_Bell Tower - English

Cast in 1679, this bronze temple bell is the oldest in Setagaya Ward —
a remarkable feat of craftsmanship that has been marking time here for over 350 years.

On New Year's Eve, its deep, resonant tones ring out across the night sky of Setagaya,
as if washing away one by one the 108 earthly desires that are said to trouble humankind.

Please take a moment to admire the deep, dignified beauty
of the ancient bronze — weathered by centuries of history
and now wearing a distinctive patina of green.

---
■ 10_Jizo Hall (Jizodo) - English

The final of the ten sacred spots is the Jizo-do — Jizo Hall —
beautifully reborn in 2020.

Enshrined here is the figure of Jizo Bodhisattva,
a compassionate guardian who watches over travelers, children,
and all living beings, offering them comfort and salvation.

Please offer your prayers here with gratitude
for completing this pilgrimage safely.

Under the warm protection of the beckoning cats,
may your future journey be filled with boundless fortune and joy.
